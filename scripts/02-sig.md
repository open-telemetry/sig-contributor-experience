# OpenTelemetry SIGs: How to Find and Join a SIG

**Target length:** 3–4 minutes
**Audience:** New OpenTelemetry contributors who want to understand and participate in Special Interest Groups.

---

## 1. Introduction — What Is a SIG? (~30 sec)

**On screen:** Open the OpenTelemetry Community repository on GitHub.

**Narration:**

Welcome back!

In this video, we'll look at one of the main ways the OpenTelemetry community organizes its work: Special Interest Groups, or SIGs.

OpenTelemetry is a large project covering specifications, language implementations, the Collector, documentation, security, contributor experience, and many other areas.

SIGs bring together contributors interested in a particular part of the project so they can collaborate, discuss ongoing work, and make progress together.

And importantly, you don't need to already be an established OpenTelemetry contributor to participate.

---

## 2. Finding the SIGs (~45 sec)

**On screen:** From the `open-telemetry/community` repository, navigate to the SIG list.

Show several categories:
- Specification SIGs
- Implementation SIGs
- Cross-Cutting SIGs

**Narration:**

The OpenTelemetry Community repository maintains a list of the project's SIGs.

You'll find groups covering specifications, implementations such as the Collector and language SDKs, and cross-cutting areas such as security, documentation, contributor experience, and developer experience.

For each SIG, you can find useful information including its meeting time, meeting notes, calendar invite group, Slack channel, related repositories, and project contacts.

For example, if you're interested in the OpenTelemetry Collector, you can find the Collector SIG here.

Or, if you're interested in helping improve the experience of contributing to OpenTelemetry, you can find the Contributor Experience SIG.

Choose the group that best matches the area you're interested in.

---

## 3. You Don't Need a Community Role to Participate (~40 sec)

**On screen:** Show the contributor documentation/community membership page, then return to the SIG list.

**Narration:**

If you're new to OpenTelemetry, you might wonder whether you need to become a member, approver, or maintainer before joining a SIG.

You don't.

OpenTelemetry has community roles with increasing levels of responsibility, but you don't need one of those roles before you start participating.

You can begin by attending meetings, listening to discussions, asking questions, contributing to issues, or helping with work that interests you.

Community roles can come later as you become more involved.

---

## 4. How to Join a SIG Meeting (~45 sec)

**On screen:** Show the SIG table and highlight:
- Meeting Time
- Meeting Invites Group
- Meeting Notes

Then show the OpenTelemetry community calendar.

**Narration:**

Once you've found a SIG you're interested in, joining its meeting is straightforward.

The SIG list shows when the group meets and provides its meeting information.

OpenTelemetry also maintains a shared community calendar containing its public meetings.

SIG meetings happen over Zoom, and the meeting notes are linked from the community documentation.

Most importantly, these meetings are open to everyone.

You don't need an invitation from a maintainer, and you don't need to have made a contribution before attending.

Find a meeting that interests you and join.

---

## 5. What Should You Do When You Join? (~60 sec)

**On screen:** Open an example SIG meeting-notes document.

Slowly show:
- agenda
- attendees, if present
- discussion items

**Narration:**

Your first SIG meeting can be as simple as listening and learning.

There's no expectation that you need to understand everything immediately or contribute to every discussion.

If the meeting agenda is editable, add your name and a short introduction to the agenda before the meeting.

When it's your turn, introduce yourself briefly. You might mention your name, what you're interested in, and what brought you to that SIG.

For example:

“Hi, I'm Abhi. I'm new to contributing to OpenTelemetry and I'm interested in the Collector and contributor experience. I'm joining today to learn more about what the SIG is working on and where I might be able to help.”

That's enough.

As you become familiar with the group, you can start participating in discussions, asking questions, picking up issues, or proposing topics for future meetings.

---

## 6. Slack Between Meetings (~30 sec)

**On screen:** Return to the SIG list and highlight the Slack Channel column.

**Narration:**

Participation doesn't stop when the meeting ends.

Each SIG listing also points you to the relevant Slack channel.

That's often the easiest place to follow discussions, ask questions, or continue a conversation from a meeting.

Joining both the meetings and the Slack channel will help you understand what the group is working on and where contributors can help.

---

## 7. Wrap-Up (~25 sec)

**On screen:** Show a simple path:

`Find a SIG → Join Slack → Join a Meeting → Introduce Yourself → Participate`

**Narration:**

So, getting involved with an OpenTelemetry SIG doesn't require a title or existing community status.

Find an area you're interested in, check its meeting information, join the Slack channel, attend a meeting, and introduce yourself.

You can start by listening and learning. As you become more familiar with the community, opportunities to contribute will follow.

We hope to see you at an OpenTelemetry SIG meeting!
